\begin{abstract}
The cost of contracting a tensor network is governed by the contraction order, and finding an
optimal order (TNCO) is NP-hard. Practice therefore relies on heuristic search ---
hyper-optimized contraction trees, simulated annealing, community detection --- whose results
can only be judged \emph{relatively}: order $A$ beats order $B$, but how far either is from the
true optimum is unknown, because no trusted \emph{absolute} yardstick exists. We supply that
yardstick: machine-checked lower bounds on the cost of \emph{every} contraction order of a
network, formalized in Lean~4 over Mathlib ($\sim$2{,}100 lines, no \texttt{sorry},
\textbf{zero custom axioms}). A trustworthy cost floor needs two independent ingredients, and
we certify both. First, a \emph{width} floor: a bramble is a combinatorial dual certificate for
pathwidth, and we prove the bramble bound self-contained via an elementary interval-Helly
argument --- the first pathwidth lower-bound theory in Lean. Second, width must genuinely
translate into cost: low-rank structure of intermediate tensors could undercut the width proxy
(this is exactly what decision-diagram methods exploit). Our \emph{Contraction Rigidity}
theorem closes this loophole structurally: any network \emph{capable} of full rank across a
cut has full rank for all tensor entries outside a proper subvariety --- no probability, and
the capability hypothesis reduces, via Kronecker routing, to a purely combinatorial cross-cut
matching. We instantiate the framework on a faithful model whose orders are genuine path
decompositions of the circuit's spacetime lattice --- widths derived, never postulated --- and
obtain, at the Sycamore-53 benchmark scale, the first contraction order carrying an absolute,
machine-checked optimality certificate: every stem order has width $\ge 27$, our explicit
sweep achieves $\le 54$. Finally we report a cautionary tale: our first iteration cited three
published graph theorems as axioms, and all three made the ambient theory inconsistent
(\texttt{False} was machine-derivable) --- a lower bound is only as absolute as its proof, and
we give the repair idiom.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
%% Narrative arc (one paragraph each):
%% 1. THE PROBLEM. TN contraction cost is dictated by the order; TNCO is NP-hard; practice
%%    is heuristic search (hyper-optimized trees / annealing / community detection /
%%    big-batch). A found order can be compared to other found orders -- and that is all.
%%    The field has RELATIVE quality judgments only.
%% 2. THE MISSING OBJECT. What is absent is an *absolute* yardstick: a trusted lower bound
%%    L such that NO contraction order costs less than L. With such a floor, any heuristic
%%    solution S acquires a certificate: cost(S)/L bounds its optimality gap. Without it,
%%    "state of the art" means "best among the orders we happened to find".
%% 3. WHY TRUST IS THE HARD PART. A cost floor has two failure modes: (i) the width bound
%%    may be wrong (graph theory subtle, proofs long); (ii) width may not translate to cost
%%    -- low-rank intermediate tensors undercut the width proxy (decision diagrams exploit
%%    exactly this). And a paper-proof floor can itself be buggy; we exhibit a real case
%%    where OUR OWN first formal floor was vacuous (inconsistent axioms prove any bound).
%%    Hence: machine-checked, axiom-free, both ingredients certified.
%% 4. THIS PAPER. Lean 4 framework delivering both ingredients + a faithful instantiation;
%%    Sycamore-53 as the canonical benchmark instance, now carrying the first absolute,
%%    machine-checked optimality gap for a contraction order (width in [27, 54]).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{An absolute yardstick for TNCO (\S\ref{sec:framework},\S\ref{sec:model}).}
    A formal cost/width model in which ``no contraction order beats bound $L$'' is a
    theorem quantified over a \emph{genuine} order space --- real path decompositions of
    the network graph, widths derived, never postulated.
  \item \textbf{Bramble certificates in Lean (\S\ref{sec:bramble}).} Brambles are
    combinatorial \emph{dual certificates} for width, playing the role LP duality plays for
    branch-and-bound floors. Stem (sweep) orders are exactly path decompositions, whose
    dual argument needs only \emph{interval} Helly --- elementary, unlike the subtree Helly
    that treewidth needs and Mathlib lacks. We prove the bramble bound self-contained and
    instantiate it on the genuine grid graph: the first pathwidth lower-bound theory in a
    proof assistant for Lean/Mathlib.
  \item \textbf{Contraction Rigidity: width $\Rightarrow$ cost, generically
    (\S\ref{sec:rigidity}).} A width floor is only a cost floor if intermediate tensors do
    not collapse in rank. We close this loophole structurally: \lstinline|BulkEntangling|
    $\Rightarrow$ \lstinline|ContractionRigid| --- one full-rank configuration upgrades to
    full rank outside a proper subvariety (Schwartz--Zippel, no probability); the
    hypothesis reduces via Kronecker routing to a combinatorial cross-cut matching.
    Random circuits become corollaries, and generic instances are fenced against
    value-structure shortcuts (the decision-diagram loophole).
  \item \textbf{The first absolutely-certified benchmark instance
    (\S\ref{sec:endtoend}).} At the Sycamore-53 scale, one machine-checked theorem pins
    every stem order to width $\ge 27$ while our explicit sweep achieves $\le 54$ ---
    an optimality-gap certificate of a factor $\le 2$ in the exponent, with
    \texttt{\#print axioms} reporting standard axioms only.
  \item \textbf{Axioms considered harmful (\S\ref{sec:axioms}).} Our first iteration cited
    three published graph theorems as global axioms over an abstract structure; all three
    are falsifiable by adversarial instances and \texttt{False} was derivable --- the
    ``certified'' floor certified nothing. We document the repair (named \lstinline|Prop|s
    supplied per model, discharged by computation) and argue it should be the default
    idiom for ``cite, don't reprove''.
  \item \textbf{Geometric routing (\S\ref{sec:routing}).} The matching hypothesis is
    realized on real hardware graphs: the $b\cdot d$ depth cap is a gate-counting theorem,
    and bond reachability is an explicit lightcone walk on 1D chains and 2D chips.
\end{itemize}

\section{The Formal Framework}\label{sec:framework}
%% Defs.lean: QTensor, cutEquiv, flatten, squareFlatten, tensorOfMatrix, bellWitness.

\section{Case Study: an Absolutely-Certified Benchmark Instance}\label{sec:endtoend}
%% Sycamore-53 as the canonical TNCO benchmark instance, now with an absolute certificate.
%% CorollaryD.lean (pure arithmetic): sweepCut 53 20 = 53; 10^18 <= 4·2^53·150 < 10^19.
%% Sycamore.lean: the four-conjunct theorem; #print axioms = standard only.
%% Read the theorem as a certificate: (i) floor -- no stem order below width 27;
%% (ii) achievability -- our sweep is a *found solution* with certified gap <= 2 in the
%% exponent; (iii) rigidity -- the width floor is a genuine cost floor for generic values.
\begin{lstlisting}
theorem sycamore53_lower_bound :
    CorollaryD.sweepCut 53 20 = 53 ∧
    ((10:ℕ)^18 ≤ CorollaryD.stemCost 53 20 150 ∧
      CorollaryD.stemCost 53 20 150 < (10:ℕ)^19) ∧
    ((∃ o, sycamore53.IsStem o ∧
        sycamore53.width o = sycamore53.cc) ∧
      (∀ o, 53 ≤ 2 * sycamore53.width o) ∧
      (∃ o, sycamore53.width o ≤ 54)) ∧
    (∃ e T, ContractionRigid leftBlock e T)
\end{lstlisting}

\section{Axioms Considered Harmful}\label{sec:axioms}
%% The CPP-audience payload. Tell it straight:
%% (a) v1 declared: axiom markovShi (G) : G.cc = G.tw;
%%     axiom latticePathwidthBound (G) (h : G.IsLattice) : G.pw <= C·G.cc;
%%     axiom treewidthLowerBound ... ;
%% (b) tw / IsLattice are *free fields* of the abstract structure -> adversarial
%%     instances falsify all three; we exhibit the three-line False derivations
%%     (machine-checked, reproduced in the appendix).
%% (c) The repair: named Props (MarkovShi G, LatticePathwidthBound G C) supplied per
%%     model; on the concrete model they are *discharged by computation* (C = 1), so the
%%     end-to-end theorem needs no axiom at all -- strictly stronger than v1 claimed.
%% (d) Lesson: "cite, don't reprove" must quantify over faithful models, never over
%%     abstract structures with free semantic fields. Hypothesis-form > axiom-form:
%%     the reader sees the citation in the statement, and #print axioms stays clean.
\begin{lstlisting}
-- v1 (UNSOUND): False was derivable
axiom latticePathwidthBound (G : CircuitGraph)
    (hlat : G.IsLattice) : G.pw ≤ C * G.cc
-- v2 (sound): a named Prop, supplied per model
def LatticePathwidthBound (G : CircuitGraph) (C : ℕ) :
    Prop := G.pw ≤ C * G.cc
\end{lstlisting}
