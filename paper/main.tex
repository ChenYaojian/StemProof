\begin{abstract}
Tensor-network contraction cost is governed by the contraction order, and finding an
optimal order (TNCO) is NP-hard, so practice relies on heuristic search. Search results
can only be judged \emph{relatively} (order $A$ beats order $B$) because no trusted
\emph{absolute} yardstick bounds the distance to the true optimum. We supply
that yardstick: machine-checked lower bounds on contraction cost, unconditional over the
\emph{sweep-style (stem)} order class, formalized in
Lean~4 over Mathlib ($\sim$2{,}800 lines, no \texttt{sorry}, \textbf{zero custom axioms});
the extension to arbitrary contraction trees rests on one explicitly-stated published
hypothesis ($\pw = \Theta(\tw)$ on lattices) and is a prose corollary, not itself formalized.
The main certified ingredient is a \emph{width} floor: brambles are combinatorial dual
certificates for pathwidth, proved self-contained via an elementary interval-Helly argument,
to our knowledge the first pathwidth lower-bound theory in Lean. A second, deliberately
elementary certificate addresses the rank-side loophole for \emph{given} parametric
families: the \emph{Contraction Rigidity} theorem
upgrades one full-rank gate configuration to a nonzero cut-determinant polynomial (the
certificate is that nonvanishing, a one-point evaluation argument with no probability
involved), with the capability
hypothesis reduced via Kronecker routing to a combinatorial cross-cut matching, so that for
such a family no low-rank (decision-diagram-style) factorization crosses the cut at any gate
parameter outside that polynomial's vanishing set. This rank-side certificate is
framework-level and secondary: it is stated alongside the cost floor as a companion theorem,
outside the headline conjunction and never composed with it; its
instantiation at the calibration cut certifies cut-size \emph{capability} only (a witness
constant in the gate parameters, available verbatim at any balanced cut); and it does not
cover concrete circuits over discrete gate sets. The calibration instance is the $53\times53$ deep-window spacetime
grid of a wire chain, whose only tie to Sycamore-53 is the shared \emph{cut scale} $53$;
there the certificate is \emph{gap-free in width}: every stem order has width $\ge 54$ and pays
total cost $\ge 2^{54}$ in the model's bag-count cost semantics, and our explicit sweep
attains width $54$: an exhibited solution carrying a machine-checked proof of optimality
among stem orders.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Contracting a tensor network is the workhorse of classical quantum-circuit simulation, and
its cost is governed almost entirely by one discrete choice: the \emph{contraction order}.
Choosing well is hard (finding an optimal order is NP-hard on general
networks~\cite{markov2008simulating}), so a decade of practice has produced a broad
ecosystem of heuristics: hyper-optimized contraction trees~\cite{gray2021hyper},
big-batch and index-slicing methods~\cite{pan2022solving}, stem-shaped orders for supremacy
circuits~\cite{huang2020classical}, multi-stem refinements~\cite{swtnc2025}, and
reuse-aware variants~\cite{kalachev2021recursive}. These tools are impressive, and they
share one methodological blind spot: \textbf{every quality judgment they support is
relative}. A new heuristic is validated by beating the previous one on a benchmark suite.
How far \emph{either} order is from the true optimum is unknown, because nothing trustworthy
bounds the optimum from below. In this regime ``state of the art'' just means the best among
the orders found so far (Fig.~\ref{fig:yardstick}).

What is missing is an \emph{absolute yardstick}: a trusted bound $L$ together with a proof
that \emph{no} contraction order costs less than $L$. With such a floor, any found order $S$
acquires a certificate ($\mathrm{cost}(S)/L$ bounds its optimality gap), exactly as an
LP dual bound certifies a branch-and-bound incumbent, or an UNSAT certificate closes a SAT
search. Fragments of an absolute reference do exist: exhaustive dynamic programming finds
guaranteed-optimal contraction sequences for small networks~\cite{pfeifer2014faster}, and
exact-treewidth solvers attach compact certificates to the width bounds they
compute~\cite{tamaki2022heuristic}. But the former stops far below the instances that
matter, and in both the guarantee rests on unverified search and checker code. A floor whose
\emph{validation} is a machine-checked theorem has not existed for TNCO.

Why not? Because a \emph{trustworthy} cost floor must survive three independent attacks, and
each attack has historically been fatal (Fig.~\ref{fig:floors}). First, the graph-theoretic
width bound underlying the floor may simply be wrong: lower-bound proofs in width theory are
subtle, and an unverified bound certifies nothing. Second, width may fail to translate into
cost: width counts indices of the largest intermediate tensor, and if that tensor has
low-rank structure, a cleverer representation pays less; decision-diagram simulators
demonstrably break the width proxy on structured instances~\cite{dd2025treewidth}. Third,
the translation must hold for the \emph{instances at hand}, not just in the worst case:
random shallow circuits are classically easy despite large width~\cite{napp2022efficient}.
Our response: make each link a named machine-checked theorem, and split the labor
accordingly — finding a good bramble (the dual certificate below) is a heuristic search
problem, while \emph{validating} one is a verified theorem. The first two attacks are closed
unconditionally for stem orders; the third is answered by a separate framework-level
certificate for \emph{generic} parametric families at a cut, which deliberately does not
cover concrete circuits over discrete gate sets (\S\ref{sec:endtoend}).

This paper delivers that yardstick for the lattice calibration family, together with a
verified certificate-validation interface that applies to any network once a bramble for it
is supplied (\S\ref{sec:limits}), formalized in Lean~4 over
Mathlib~\cite{moura2021lean,mathlib2020}: $\sim$2{,}800 lines, 18 modules (17 proof
modules plus a build-time axiom-audit module), no
\texttt{sorry}, zero custom axioms; \texttt{\#print axioms} of every theorem below
reports only \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, \texttt{Quot.sound}. The
unconditional floors quantify over all \emph{sweep-style (stem)} orders, the class that
state-vector-like simulators and stem-shaped supremacy strategies actually
populate~\cite{huang2020classical}. The extension to arbitrary contraction trees rests on a
single explicitly-stated published hypothesis ($\pw = \Theta(\tw)$ on lattices); the
development contains no type of tree contraction orders, so that conditional statement is a
literature corollary quoted in prose, not a Lean theorem (\S\ref{sec:limits}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{An absolute yardstick for TNCO (\S\ref{sec:framework},\S\ref{sec:model}).}
    A formal cost/width model in which ``no stem (sweep) contraction order beats bound
    $L$'' is a theorem quantified over a \emph{genuine} order space (real path decompositions of
    the network graph, widths derived, never postulated) with an explicit cost object:
    \lstinline|orderCost| sums the materialized boundary tensors, and the floor is read off
    as a \emph{cost} statement (\lstinline|gridModel_cost_floor|:
    $2^{N+1} \le \mathrm{cost}(o)$ for every order $o$ of the $N \times N$ lattice, a
    direct corollary of the width floor, made explicit so the yardstick prices cost).
  \item \textbf{Bramble certificates in Lean (\S\ref{sec:bramble}).} Brambles are
    combinatorial \emph{dual certificates} for width, playing the role LP duality plays for
    branch-and-bound floors: however a bramble is found (by hand, by heuristic search),
    the checked theorem turns it into an absolute floor. Stem (sweep) orders are exactly
    path decompositions, whose dual argument needs only \emph{interval} Helly:
    elementary, unlike the subtree Helly that treewidth needs and Mathlib lacks. We prove
    the bound for genuine \emph{touching} brambles (blocks intersecting \emph{or} adjacent)
    and instantiate two: the elementary crosses (order $\Theta(k)$) and the augmented
    Seymour--Thomas-style bramble (\S\ref{sec:exact}), whose order $k+2$ pins the grid
    floor \emph{exactly}. To our knowledge, the first pathwidth lower-bound theory in
    Lean/Mathlib.
  \item \textbf{Contraction Rigidity: a deliberately elementary, framework-level
    no-compression certificate (\S\ref{sec:rigidity}).} A width floor prices cost only if intermediate tensors do
    not collapse in rank. We address this loophole with a separate, self-standing
    certificate, \lstinline|BulkEntangling|
    $\Rightarrow$ \lstinline|ContractionRigid|: one full-rank configuration makes the cut
    determinant a nonzero polynomial, confining rank collapse to that polynomial's vanishing
    set, with no probability involved. The engine is deliberately elementary, and so is the
    certificate's logical content: evaluation commutes with determinants, so
    \lstinline|ContractionRigid| is equivalent to the nonvanishing of the determinant
    polynomial alone (\S\ref{sec:rigidity}); the packaging carries the
    value: the hypothesis reduces via Kronecker routing to a combinatorial cross-cut
    matching, and the payoff is a formal \emph{no-compression theorem}
    (\lstinline|ContractionRigid.no_compression|): for a rigid parametric family, outside
    the vanishing set of one nonzero polynomial the cut flattening admits
    no factorization with inner dimension below $2^k$, i.e.\ no MPS-style bond and no
    rank-revealing compression (the decision-diagram loophole). We rank this layer below the
    width floor --- its mathematical content is a one-point evaluation argument --- and its
    scope is bounded three ways. The
    measure-zero reading of that vanishing set is stated in prose only
    (\S\ref{sec:limits}); the theorem is \emph{not} composed with the cost floor of
    \S\ref{sec:model} (the two certificates are proved as separate theorems and never
    composed, \S\ref{sec:endtoend}); and the certificate constrains a \emph{given} family $T$:
    its existential instantiation at the benchmark cut is discharged by a Bell-type
    witness available at any balanced cut, carrying no architecture information
    (\S\ref{sec:endtoend}).
  \item \textbf{A calibration instance certified gap-free in width (\S\ref{sec:endtoend}).}
    On the $53\times53$ deep-window spacetime grid (the calibration instance at the Sycamore-53
    \emph{cut scale}; \S\ref{sec:endtoend} states exactly how it relates to the $(53,20)$
    benchmark parameters), one machine-checked theorem pins the optimum exactly:
    $\cc = 54$ (\lstinline|sycamore53_cc_exact|), every stem order pays total cost
    $\ge 2^{54}$ (\lstinline|sycamore53_cost_floor|), and the explicit sweep attains the
    width floor: an exhibited solution carrying a machine-checked \emph{optimality proof},
    with \texttt{\#print axioms} reporting standard axioms only. Gap-freeness is a
    \emph{width} statement: the cost floor inherits the width floor's exponent, not the
    sweep's step count, and the sweep's own total cost is $53^2\cdot 2^{54}$-scale
    (\S\ref{sec:endtoend}). A companion theorem kept outside this conjunction records the
    rank side: an explicit witness family built from the cross-cut matching (constant in
    the gate parameters, identity bonds along the block-aligned matching) attains full rank
    at the $53$-qubit cut, certifying the cut's entangling \emph{capability} only
    (\lstinline|sycamore53_bond_floor| then bounds any crossing factorization of that
    witness below $2^{53}$).
  \item \textbf{Geometric routing scaffolding (\S\ref{sec:routing}).} Combinatorial
    support for the matching hypothesis: the $b\cdot d$ depth cap is a counting theorem
    about abstract gate schedules, and bond reachability is an explicit bounded-length
    walk in the chain and chip \emph{coupling graphs}. No circuit-dynamics model is
    formalized, and the rigidity witnesses of \S\ref{sec:endtoend} do not consume these
    theorems: they scope, but do not discharge, the physical routing step.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background}\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Tensor networks, orders, and width}
A quantum circuit compiles into a tensor network: one tensor per gate, one shared index per
wire. Simulating the circuit means contracting the network to a scalar, and a
\emph{contraction order} schedules the pairwise merges. The cost of an order is dominated by
its widest intermediate tensor: an intermediate with $w$ open indices holds $2^w$ entries.
Markov and Shi~\cite{markov2008simulating} identified the optimal width over all orders with
the treewidth of the network's line graph, which pins the complexity of the
\emph{contraction paradigm} (including index slicing, which trades memory for time but not
total work). The paradigm bound carries two caveats that scope everything below. It is not an
unconditional hardness claim: representations exploiting \emph{value} structure
(stabilizer states, decision diagrams~\cite{dd2025treewidth}) can beat width on structured
instances, and shallow random 2D circuits are classically easy outright%
~\cite{napp2022efficient}. Our floors therefore live in the contraction paradigm, and
\S\ref{sec:rigidity} shows that for \emph{generic} tensor entries even the value-structure
escape is closed for rigid parametric families at a cut (a separate companion certificate,
never composed with the cost floor). Also, NP-hardness of TNCO is a statement about general networks; on
planar grids the width is textbook-known, which is precisely what will qualify the
lattice family as a \emph{calibration} class in \S\ref{sec:endtoend}.

\subsection{Stem orders are path decompositions}
The orders that dominate practice on supremacy-style circuits are
\emph{stems}~\cite{huang2020classical}: a single growing tensor absorbs the network one
piece at a time, exactly like a state vector swept through a circuit. In tree language the
contraction tree is a caterpillar: a spine with leaves. The combinatorial identity at
the heart of this paper (Fig.~\ref{fig:stemsweep}) is that \textbf{a stem order is the same
data as a path decomposition} of the network graph: the $t$-th bag $B_t$ is the boundary of
the absorbed region after $t$ steps, the width of the order is the largest bag, and the
optimal stem width is the pathwidth. This identity is what makes certified floors for stem
orders tractable today: pathwidth lower bounds rest on \emph{interval} Helly
(\S\ref{sec:bramble}), a one-dimensional argument, where treewidth would need the subtree
Helly property that Mathlib does not yet have.

\subsection{The sweep cut and the benchmark scale}
For an $n$-qubit depth-$d$ lattice circuit the two canonical sweeps give cut sizes $n$
(time sweep) and $\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor\cdot d$ (space sweep), so the achievable width
scale is $\mathrm{sweepCut}(n,d)=\min(n,\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor d)$; at the Sycamore-53
parameters $(n,d)=(53,20)$~\cite{arute2019quantum} this is $53$, and the folklore cost estimate
$4\cdot 2^{53}\cdot 150 \approx 10^{18}$ (with the step count $150$ taken as an input
from that folklore estimate, not derived) is pure arithmetic that our development also
certifies (\lstinline|CorollaryD|). Throughout, ``Sycamore-53 scale'' refers to the cut
value these parameters fix; the lattice we instantiate is the $53\times53$
\emph{deep-window} spacetime grid of a wire chain, whose depth window ($53$ layers)
exceeds the benchmark depth ($20$); \S\ref{sec:endtoend} states which certified
conjunct speaks about which object.

\subsection{Lean 4 and Mathlib}
The development is built on Lean~4 and Mathlib (both pinned at v4.30.0), using
\lstinline|SimpleGraph| box products for lattices, \lstinline|Finset| combinatorics for
brambles and transversals, and \lstinline|MvPolynomial| with matrix determinants for the
genericity engine. The auditing interface is \texttt{\#print axioms}: every theorem cited in
this paper reports \texttt{[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]}; no custom axiom
exists anywhere in the library, and the one literature input we rely on
($\pw=\Theta(\tw)$ on lattices) appears as an explicit hypothesis \emph{inside} theorem
statements, never as an ambient assumption.

\section{The Formal Framework}\label{sec:framework}

The development separates cleanly into a linear-algebra half and a combinatorial half, and
the design decision that keeps both halves simple is that neither imports the other's
machinery: the linear-algebra half is organized around one interface, the
\emph{flattening of a tensor across a cut}, which the combinatorial half never needs;
the two halves meet only in the conjunction of \S\ref{sec:endtoend}.

On the linear-algebra side, a tensor on legs $I$ is just an array indexed by one bit per
leg, and a cut is a predicate $p$ on legs; flattening reshapes the tensor into a matrix
whose rows are assignments to the $p$-side and whose columns are assignments to the rest:

\begin{lstlisting}
abbrev QTensor (I R : Type*) := (I → Fin 2) → R

def flatten (T : QTensor I R) :
    Matrix ({i // p i} → Fin 2) ({i // ¬ p i} → Fin 2) R :=
  Matrix.of fun a b => T ((cutEquiv p).symm (a, b))
\end{lstlisting}

The rank of this matrix is the Schmidt rank across the cut, and ``no rank collapse''
(the property \S\ref{sec:rigidity} certifies) is exactly nonsingularity of its square
form at a balanced cut. One lemma does all the transport work: entrywise evaluation
commutes with flattening (\lstinline|flatten_map|), so any genericity statement proved for
polynomial entries descends to every concrete evaluation.

On the combinatorial side, a circuit graph is modelled by its space of contraction orders:
a type \lstinline|Order|, a derived \lstinline|width| per order, a predicate
\lstinline|IsStem|, and the two optima $\cc$ (infimum of width over all orders) and $\pw$
(over stem orders). The proved skeleton is small and unconditional: $\cc \le \pw$, the stem
optimum is attained, and \emph{given} a bound $\pw \le C\cdot\cc$ there is a stem order
within factor $C$ of optimal. The literature inputs are deliberately \emph{not} axioms:
\lstinline|MarkovShi G| and \lstinline|LatticePathwidthBound G C| are named
\lstinline|Prop|s that a caller must supply for the concrete model at hand. On the model of
\S\ref{sec:model} the latter holds trivially (that model's order space contains only stem
orders, so the bound compares an infimum with itself), and no theorem in this paper carries
an unproved ambient assumption; the genuine tree-order content is precisely what that model
cannot express (\S\ref{sec:limits}).

\section{Contraction Rigidity}\label{sec:rigidity}

A width floor certifies cost only if the widest intermediate tensor genuinely carries
$2^w$ degrees of freedom. The literature treats this as an average-case question
(``Haar-random circuits have full-rank cuts with high probability'') and answers it with
random-matrix analysis, whose guarantees are moreover regime-sensitive: shallow random 2D
circuits are classically easy outright~\cite{napp2022efficient}.
We replace the probabilistic question with a structural one, and the answer becomes a
two-line corollary of one-point polynomial nonvanishing.

Say a parameterized tensor family $T$ (entries are polynomials in the gate parameters) is
\lstinline|BulkEntangling| at a balanced cut if \emph{some} parameter point makes the cut
flattening nonsingular: the architecture is \emph{capable} of full Schmidt rank. Say it
is \lstinline|ContractionRigid| if the flattening's determinant is a nonzero polynomial and
the flattening is nonsingular at every parameter outside that polynomial's vanishing set.
The structural theorem is that capability upgrades to genericity:

\begin{lstlisting}
theorem contraction_rigidity
    (e : ({i // p i} → Fin 2) ≃ ({i // ¬ p i} → Fin 2))
    {T : QTensor I (MvPolynomial ι K)}
    (h : BulkEntangling p e T) : ContractionRigid p e T
\end{lstlisting}

The engine is elementary (a determinant that is nonzero at one evaluation is a nonzero
polynomial), and one bookkeeping fact should be stated plainly. Because evaluation commutes
with determinants (\lstinline|det_squareFlatten_map|, an unconditional identity), the second
clause of \lstinline|ContractionRigid| (nonsingularity at every parameter outside the
vanishing set) holds for \emph{every} family once the first does, so
\lstinline|ContractionRigid| is logically equivalent to its first clause alone: the
flattening's determinant is a nonzero polynomial. The pointwise clause is kept in the
definition because downstream consumers (\lstinline|no_compression| below) use it in exactly
that form. Properness of the vanishing set (a nonempty complement, over an infinite field)
is proved separately (\lstinline|exists_nonsingular_of_det_ne_zero|) and is not part of
the certificate. No probability enters; the measure-zero corollary
($\Pr[\text{rank collapse}]=0$ for any continuous gate measure) is a one-step consequence we
leave to future work.

What makes the theorem usable is that its hypothesis is \emph{combinatorial}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:matching}). If the $k$ qubits on each side of the cut can be paired up by a
perfect matching whose pairs each carry a nonsingular two-qubit bond, then the flattening
at the witness configuration is the Kronecker product of the bonds, and a product of
nonsingular blocks is nonsingular (\lstinline|det_bondProd_ne_zero|, by induction with
\lstinline|det_kronecker|). Checking \lstinline|BulkEntangling| for an architecture thus
reduces to exhibiting a cross-cut matching, a graph condition whose hardware-graph side
\S\ref{sec:routing} scopes (without discharging the physical routing step). The hypothesis is not vacuous for real gate sets: the
development proves $\det \neq 0$ for the standard library ($X, Y, Z, H, S, T, \sqrt{X},
\mathrm{CZ}, \mathrm{SWAP}, \mathrm{CNOT}, \mathrm{iSWAP}, \mathrm{fSim}(\theta,\phi)$,
\ldots), so identity bonds, Bell pairs, and Sycamore's native $\mathrm{fSim}$ all qualify.
One boundary must be drawn precisely: the theorem constrains a \emph{given} family $T$ (the
tensors of a fixed circuit). The bare existential $\exists T$ used in the companion
capability theorem of
\S\ref{sec:endtoend} is, by contrast, discharged at \emph{any} balanced cut by the Bell
witness, so it certifies cut-size capability only and never a property of a particular
architecture.

\paragraph{From rank to compression: the no-compression theorem.}
Rigidity complements the cost story with a final, self-standing step. If the cut flattening $M$ factors as
$M = A\cdot B$ through an inner index set of size $r$ (an MPS-style bond, a
decision-diagram node cut, any rank-revealing compression), then $r$ bounds the rank of
$M$ from above. Formally, for a rigid family and any parameter outside the vanishing set:

\begin{lstlisting}
theorem ContractionRigid.no_compression ... :
    squareFlatten p e (fun x => eval v (T x)) = A * B →
    2 ^ Fintype.card {i // p i} ≤ r
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent (proved via injectivity of \lstinline|M.mulVec| and monotonicity of
\lstinline|finrank| under injections). For a rigid parametric family, therefore, \emph{no}
representation crosses the cut with inner dimension below $2^k$ outside the vanishing set of
one nonzero polynomial (measure zero for any continuous gate measure, a reading we state
but do not formalize, \S\ref{sec:limits}). Structured instances (stabilizer
circuits, genuinely low-rank tensors) live inside that vanishing set and can beat the
floor; that is the intended semantics of a generic-instance certificate. One
scope note: \lstinline|no_compression| speaks about a parametric tensor family at a cut; no
theorem links it to the \lstinline|orderCost| of \S\ref{sec:model}. The compression floor
and the cost floor are certified as \emph{separate} theorems in \S\ref{sec:endtoend};
neither composes with the other.

\section{Stem Width via Interval Helly}\label{sec:bramble}

The width floor is a pathwidth lower bound, and its formal interface is deliberately
minimal. A candidate stem order is a \lstinline|LinearBags| (a finite sequence of bags)
together with the three defining axioms of a path decomposition: each vertex's bags
form a contiguous interval (\lstinline|vint|), every edge lies in some bag
(\lstinline|edge|), and every vertex is covered (\lstinline|cov|). Everything a floor needs
is derived from these three.

The dual certificate is a \emph{bramble}: a family of connected, pairwise-touching vertex
blocks. Its \emph{order} is the least size of a transversal (a set meeting every block),
and the bound to prove is that the largest bag of any path decomposition is at least the
order. The proof (Fig.~\ref{fig:helly}) is where the pathwidth restriction pays off.
Connectivity plus the edge-cover axiom forces each block to meet the bags along a
contiguous \emph{interval} of indices (\lstinline|bag_meets_betweenness|, an induction on
walks); pairwise touching forces those intervals to pairwise overlap; and pairwise
overlapping intervals share a common point: the interval Helly property, whose whole
proof is ``take the largest left endpoint''
(\lstinline|interval_helly|, six lines). The bag at the common index meets every block, so
it is a transversal, and its size bounds the order. For treewidth the same argument needs
the Helly property of subtrees, which Mathlib does not have; for pathwidth the
one-dimensional case suffices, which is what puts certified stem floors within reach of
current Mathlib.

Two refinements make the bound usable beyond toy brambles. First, blocks may
\emph{touch without intersecting} (the genuine bramble condition allows adjacency),
and then the shared index comes from the edge-cover axiom rather than a shared vertex
(\lstinline|pathwidth_ge_order_of_touching|). Second, the whole development is indexed by
an arbitrary finite type, so structured brambles (\S\ref{sec:exact}) index naturally.
Instantiating with the elementary \emph{crosses} bramble (block $i$ is row $i$ united
with column $i$, connected in the genuine grid graph via box-product walk embeddings)
already yields a fully unconditional $\Theta(k)$ floor:
\begin{figure}[t]

\section{The Exact Floor: an Augmented Bramble}\label{sec:exact}

For asymptotics, $\Theta(k)$ is the end of the story; for a \emph{yardstick} it is not.
A floor of $k/2$ against a sweep of $k+1$ certifies only ``within a factor two in the
exponent'', a weak certificate, and an unnecessary one, because the slack is entirely in
the bramble. The crosses bramble genuinely has order $\lceil k/2\rceil$: one vertex
$(i,j)$ hits two crosses at once. Closing the gap needs a better dual witness, the
augmented bramble in the style of Seymour and Thomas~\cite{seymour1993graph}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:augmented}): on the $(k{+}1)\times(k{+}1)$ grid, take the $k^2$ crosses of
the $k\times k$ subgrid, plus the last row, plus the last column minus the corner.

The boundary blocks are \emph{disjoint}
from every cross (they only touch through edges), which is exactly what the
touching-bramble bound of \S\ref{sec:bramble} was built for. Connectivity of the
subgrid crosses needs walks that do not overshoot the subgrid; we build ascending chain
walks with explicit support bounds and route both arms to the cross centre. And the
transversal count is the classical ``miss a row and miss a column'' argument, formalized by
projections: a transversal must contain a last-row vertex and a last-column vertex (two
distinct vertices no cross contains), and its subgrid part must cover all $k$ subgrid rows
or all $k$ subgrid columns (otherwise some cross $(i_0, j_0)$ is unhit), so the order
is at least $k+2$:

\section{A Faithful Circuit Model}\label{sec:model}

A floor is only as meaningful as the order space it quantifies over, and formalizations are
most vulnerable exactly here: it is easy to prove strong statements about a model that
postulates its own numbers. Our first model did (a single abstract order with constant
width), and we report that dead end because its repair is the methodological point: in
the final model \textbf{no width or cost number is postulated} (two inert record fields,
\lstinline|tw| and \lstinline|IsLattice|, remain for interface compatibility and are
consumed by no theorem cited in this paper). An order \emph{is} a path decomposition of
the genuine grid graph (the three axioms of \S\ref{sec:bramble}, bundled as
\lstinline|IsPathDecomp|), its width \emph{is} its largest bag, and its cost \emph{is} the
total size of the boundary tensors it materializes,
$\mathrm{cost}(o)=\sum_t 2^{|B_t|}$ (\lstinline|orderCost|).

\begin{lstlisting}
def gridModel (k : ℕ) : CircuitGraph where
  Order  := {L : LinearBags (Fin k × Fin k) // IsPathDecomp k L}
  width  := fun o => bagsWidth o.1
  IsStem := fun _ => True
  ...
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent Here \lstinline|IsStem| $\equiv$ \lstinline|True| is definitional rather than
degenerate: this order space consists exactly of the linear (stem) orders, and quantifying
over tree orders as well is precisely the $\pw=\Theta(\tw)$ literature input, carried as a
hypothesis where used.

The floor is \S\ref{sec:exact}'s theorem, now speaking about every order of the model. The
matching upper bound is the explicit sliding-window sweep (Fig.~\ref{fig:window}): absorb
vertices in column-major position order and let the bag at step $t$ be the window of
positions $[t, t+k]$, the live frontier plus the vertex being absorbed. The window
satisfies all three decomposition axioms (the interval property is arithmetic on positions;
every grid edge spans a position difference of $1$ or $k$, so the window at the smaller
endpoint covers it) and holds at most $k+1$ vertices, positions being injective. Floor and
sweep meet, so the model's optimum is pinned exactly and the cost floor follows from
$2^{\mathrm{width}} \le \mathrm{cost}$:

\begin{lstlisting}
theorem gridModel_cc_eq (N : ℕ) (hN : 2 ≤ N) :
    (gridModel N).cc = N + 1

theorem gridModel_cost_floor (N : ℕ) (hN : 2 ≤ N)
    (o : (gridModel N).Order) : 2 ^ (N + 1) ≤ orderCost o
\end{lstlisting}

\noindent The optimum is moreover \emph{attained} (via \lstinline|Nat.sInf_mem|), and
\lstinline|LatticePathwidthBound (gridModel N) 1| holds by construction (trivially so,
since this order space contains only stems, \S\ref{sec:framework}), so every
hypothesis-form theorem of \S\ref{sec:framework} applies to this model with no residual
hypothesis (the genuine tree-order content stays untouched as the literature input of
\S\ref{sec:limits}), and the axiom footprint stays at exactly the three standard axioms.

\section{Case Study: a Benchmark Instance Certified Gap-Free in Width}\label{sec:endtoend}

Why calibrate on a lattice? Precisely because lattices are \emph{not} the hard case: their
widths are textbook-known, so they are the one instance family where the yardstick's reading
can be checked against independent truth. Instantiating the model at $N = 53$ (the
deep-window spacetime grid of a 53-wire chain), one theorem conjoins the whole certificate.
The instance's only tie to the Sycamore-53 benchmark is the shared cut value $53$ fixed by
conjunct~(i) below; the Lean prefix \lstinline|sycamore53| names this calibration instance,
not the physical device:

\begin{lstlisting}
theorem sycamore53_lower_bound :
    CorollaryD.sweepCut 53 20 = 53 ∧
    ((10:ℕ)^18 ≤ CorollaryD.stemCost 53 20 150 ∧
      CorollaryD.stemCost 53 20 150 < (10:ℕ)^19) ∧
    (sycamore53.cc = 54 ∧
      (∃ o, sycamore53.IsStem o ∧
        sycamore53.width o = sycamore53.cc) ∧
      (∀ o : sycamore53.Order,
        2 ^ 54 ≤ GridModel.orderCost o))
\end{lstlisting}

Read as a certificate, the three conjuncts say: (i)~the sweep-cut arithmetic behind the
folklore $10^{18}$ estimate is exact; (ii)~the optimum over \emph{all} stem orders of the
model's spacetime grid graph is $54$ (the floor of \S\ref{sec:exact} meets the sweep of
\S\ref{sec:model}), so the sweep is an exhibited solution carrying a machine-checked
\emph{optimality proof}; (iii)~every order pays total cost at least $2^{54}$.
\texttt{\#print axioms} on the theorem reports
\texttt{[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]}.

\paragraph{The rank-side companion.} The rank side is deliberately \emph{not} a conjunct of
this theorem: its logical content is thinner than the conjuncts above, and folding it into
the headline conjunction would let it borrow their weight. A companion theorem
(\lstinline|sycamore53_bond_floor|) exhibits a witness tensor family, built from the
cross-cut matching, that is contraction rigid at the $53$-qubit cut; for that family,
\lstinline|no_compression| yields that no factorization with inner dimension below $2^{53}$
crosses the cut. This is an existence statement about the cut's entangling capacity, not a
rank claim about any specific benchmark circuit; the witness family is \emph{constant} in
the gate parameters (identity bonds along the block-aligned matching), so its
\lstinline|ContractionRigid| holds with an empty exceptional set. Stronger still: the
existential is discharged by a Bell-type witness that exists at \emph{every} balanced cut
of \emph{every} index type, so the companion carries no information specific to this
instance beyond the cut size $53$. What it certifies is \emph{capability} at that size; the
generic quantifier does no additional work on this witness, and the substantive rigidity
statement remains the framework theorem for a \emph{given} circuit family
(\S\ref{sec:rigidity}).

Fidelity scope. The three conjuncts and the rank-side companion do not all speak about one
physical object. Conjuncts (i)--(ii) are arithmetic at the benchmark parameters $(53,20)$, whose
cut value $53$ arises from the \emph{two-dimensional chip} geometry
($\lfloor\sqrt{n}\rfloor$ cross-cut gates per layer). Conjunct (iii) certifies the
$53\times53$ spacetime grid of a \emph{wire chain}: a deep window of $53$ layers,
exceeding the benchmark depth $20$. At depth $20$ a wire chain's spacetime grid is
$53\times20$, its stem scale is governed by the depth ($\approx 21$), and by the
saturation criterion of \S\ref{sec:routing} it lies below the deep-regime threshold. A
further modeling step sits underneath (iii): the certified graph is the full $53\times53$
grid graph (the box product of two paths), a proxy geometry for the sparser gate-level
network graph of a depth-$53$ brickwork chain --- the two agree at the $\Theta$ scale but
can differ by constants, and only the grid graph is what the theorem quantifies over. What
(iii) certifies is thus the deep-regime \emph{calibration} instance at the Sycamore-53 cut
scale, not the $(53,20)$ chain and not the $(2{+}1)$-dimensional lattice of the
physical chip. The two cost readings are likewise distinct measures and do not reconcile
numerically: conjunct~(ii) prices $150$ folklore stem steps in bond semantics
($4\cdot 2^{53}\cdot 150 \approx 5\times 10^{18}$), while conjunct~(iii)'s
\lstinline|orderCost| charges each of the model's $53^2 = 2809$ bag steps $2^{|B_t|}$
entries, so the explicit sweep's own \lstinline|orderCost| is on the order of
$53^2\cdot 2^{54} \approx 5\times 10^{19}$, outside conjunct~(ii)'s window. Neither
number bounds the other's object, and a claim made in one semantics must not be read in the
other. The companion capability theorem is stated in generic form: the concrete Sycamore circuit draws its
single-qubit gates from a discrete set, which a vanishing-set (generic) statement does not
cover: the certificate makes no rank claim about that specific instance. The matching side of
the certificate already covers two-dimensional chips (\S\ref{sec:routing}); the
chip-lattice width floor is future work (\S\ref{sec:limits}), and nothing in this section
depends on it.

\section{Empirical Demonstration: Reading the Yardstick}\label{sec:empirical}

We held the yardstick against a real search.
For each $N$ in a sweep up to the benchmark scale we built the closed $N\times N$ grid
tensor network (all bond dimensions $2$) and ran cotengra's
hyper-optimizer~\cite{gray2021hyper} (v0.7.1, \texttt{minimize=size}, $128$ repeats per
instance, single CPU node, $\sim$22 minutes total; a budget-sensitivity rerun of the
drift instances at $512$ and $4096$ repeats is reported below), recording the best found
contraction width ($\log_2$ of the largest intermediate) and comparing it against the certified
stem scale $N+1$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:gapplot}). One semantic note scopes the comparison:
the found orders are \emph{trees} measured in \emph{bond} count, while the certified floor
is a statement about \emph{stem} orders in \emph{vertex-bag} semantics, and the two
measures agree only up to an additive unit: a vertex-by-vertex frontier carries the one
extra bond of the vertex being absorbed, which a tree merge can pre-contract away.
(Concretely, on the closed grid: mid-column the sweep's intermediate exposes the $N$
cross-column bonds plus the one intra-column bond at the absorption point, so its bond
width is $N{+}1$, matching the bag count; pre-contracting the entering vertex is worth
exactly that one unit.) Claims
at $\pm 1$ granularity \emph{across} the two semantics are therefore not licensed by our
theorems; what the theory predicts is $|\text{found} - (N{+}1)| \le 1 + \text{search
slack}$. The prediction is a genuine test because on lattices trees should gain nothing
substantial over stems at all: $\pw = \tw$ on grids is exactly the content of the
stem-existence thesis (the explicitly-carried hypothesis of \S\ref{sec:limits}), so a
search that is free to return \emph{arbitrary} trees ought to land within one conversion
unit of the stem line, and never substantially below it.

The data falls into three regimes. In the first, the data is consistent with the
stem-existence thesis: an unrestricted tree search, free to return any contraction
tree whatsoever, \emph{never} beats the stem line by more than the one-unit semantics
allowance, exactly what ``optimal contraction of lattice networks can be taken
stem-shaped'' predicts, and the two $-1$ points are the conversion residue, not a
refutation. In the second regime, on eight instances ($8 \le N \le 28$, and again at
$N = 48$), the found width coincides numerically with the certified
scale: agreement at the granularity the two semantics license ($\pm 1$), and to our
knowledge the first time heuristic contraction orders come with an absolute confirmation
of their scale rather than a comparison to other heuristics.
In the third, at $N \in \{32, 36, 44, 53\}$, the fixed-budget search ($128$ repeats,
deliberately modest; production settings run orders of magnitude more) drifts $+1$ to $+2$
\emph{above} the scale: at $N=36$ the returned order is a factor $4$ in tensor size
away from what the certified line says is achievable, and nothing in a relative-only
methodology would ever flag it. The drift measures search quality \emph{at this budget},
not the hyper-optimizer's limit behavior. The yardstick makes the follow-up
question quantitative, so we asked it: rerunning the drift points at $512$ and $4096$
repeats, every found width returns to within the conversion allowance of the certified
line ($N \in \{32, 44\}$: gap $0$ at $512$; $N=36$: $-1$; $N=53$: $+1$ at $512$ and
exactly $54 = N+1$ at $4096$), and no point ever falls below the lower edge of that
allowance. As the budget grows, the found widths converge into the conversion allowance
around the certified scale and never fall below its lower edge.

\paragraph{From calibration grids to RQC architectures.}
The grid family calibrates the yardstick; random-quantum-circuit \emph{architectures} are
where the stem thesis must actually live, so we ran the same protocol on gate-level
spacetime networks of RQC families (single closed amplitude, one rank-$4$ tensor per
two-qubit gate): 1D brickwork chains in the deep regime ($L = 2n$ layers), a depth
transition at fixed $n=24$, and 2D chips with the Sycamore-style ABCD coupler
pattern~\cite{arute2019quantum}. Contraction width depends only on the network graph, so
these are exactly the graphs of random circuits; for each instance we also \emph{exhibit}
two genuine stem orders (the time sweep and the space sweep) and compute their exact
widths, making the comparison ``free tree search vs.\ the stem the theory prescribes''
(Table~\ref{tab:rqc}).

Across all twenty-five deep-regime instances, \textbf{no found
tree beats an exhibited stem}: the stem thesis, tested on genuine RQC graphs, exactly
on its stated domain. The domain matters physically and formally: below threshold the
entanglement lightcone has not yet connected all qubits, the cut is governed by the
lightcone width rather than by $n$, and neither the floor nor the thesis is being tested.
This is Napp's simulable phase~\cite{napp2022efficient}, and its boundary is marked by
the lightcone-walk theorem of \S\ref{sec:routing} (\lstinline|bond_causal|: an explicit walk
certifies that the $i$-th cross-cut pair is reachable whenever $i < d$; the converse
direction is not formalized). The shallow entries at $-2$ sit outside
the domain and, secondarily, measure the slack of our two canonical witnesses there.
Next, the $n{=}24$ depth sweep traces the entry into the deep regime cleanly: the
crossover sits exactly where the space sweep overtakes the time sweep
($L \approx 26 \approx n$), i.e.\ where the lightcone first spans the chain; and once
saturated the stem scale is depth-independent: doubling $L{=}2n$ to $3n$ leaves the
stem at $n$ while the search drifts further. Finally, on the
Sycamore-class 2D architectures the free search returns trees $+2$ to $+17$ units
\emph{above} the trivial time-sweep stem, and the deficit \emph{grows with scale}: at
$7{\times}7$ chips ($49$ qubits, the Sycamore-53 size class) the returned order
materializes tensors $2^{17} \approx 10^{5}\times$ larger than the stem that theory hands
over for free. On supremacy-class architectures the searcher fails to \emph{find} the
stem, and the deficit is largest at the very scales the
benchmark targets. Nor do larger budgets rescue it: at $512$ and $4096$ repeats ($4\times$ and
$32\times$) the deficit shrinks only to $+10$ and then $+6$ on the $6{\times}6$ chip, and
to $+16$/$+10$ on the $7{\times}7$, $12$-cycle instance, still $2^{6}$--$2^{10}$ times
the stem's tensor size after a $32\times$ budget increase, closing at roughly four width
units per $8\times$ budget. In budget terms, the search closes on a stem that the theory
supplies for free only logarithmically --- a direct practical argument for certified
stem-aware ordering.

\section{Geometric Routing: Brickwork and Lightcones}\label{sec:routing}

The rigidity certificate of \S\ref{sec:rigidity} consumes a cross-cut matching, and a
skeptic should ask whether real hardware ever supplies one of the required size. The
development answers with two combinatorial theorems about brickwork circuits, keeping the
physics out of the trusted base. One is a counting cap: a depth-$d$ schedule whose layers
each place at most $b$ gates across the cut can route at most $b\cdot d$ distinct bonds
(proved, not assumed: \lstinline|routed_card_le|, a \lstinline|card_biUnion| bound), and
$\min(n, b\cdot d)$ is achieved by an explicit schedule. The other is causal reachability: bonds
are not abstract labels but qubit pairs that must lie within each other's depth-$d$
lightcone. On a chain, an explicit walk of length $2i+1$ joins the $i$-th nested cross-cut
pair, placing it within the depth-$d$ lightcone whenever $i < d$ (\lstinline|bond_causal|;
the converse (unreachability for $i \ge d$, a coupling-graph distance lower bound) is
not formalized); on a
two-dimensional chip the chain walks lift through the box-product structure row by row,
giving a causally-realizable matching of size $\min(n/2, \sqrt{n}\,d)$ on square chips,
the $\mathrm{sweepCut}$ scale again. Together these \emph{scope} the physical routing step:
the coupling graphs of deep brickwork layouts contain cross-cut pairings of the required
size, each within lightcone distance. Two caveats bound this claim. No
circuit-dynamics model is formalized: ``a depth-$d$ circuit can entangle a pair within
its lightcone'' is a modeling correspondence, and the walk theorems above are statements
about coupling-graph distance. And the rigidity witnesses of \S\ref{sec:endtoend} do
not consume these theorems: they take the block-aligned matching directly as data. The
concrete $(2{+}1)$-dimensional ABCD coupler schedule of the physical chip is a further
fidelity step we have not formalized.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{TNCO in practice.} Hyper-optimized contraction trees~\cite{gray2021hyper},
big-batch contraction~\cite{pan2022solving}, stem orders~\cite{huang2020classical},
multi-stem scheduling~\cite{swtnc2025}, and reuse-aware search~\cite{kalachev2021recursive}
define the state of the art; treewidth estimates for circuit graphs go back to
QuickBB-style heuristics~\cite{boixo2017simulation}. All quality judgments in this line are
relative; none produces a certificate against the optimum. The one exception proves the
rule: exhaustive dynamic programming returns guaranteed-optimal sequences for small
networks~\cite{pfeifer2014faster}, but the guarantee rests on the search implementation and
the method stops where the instances start to matter. Our work is complementary: it
does not search for orders; it bounds from below the cost of the orders a search returns.

\paragraph{Width theory.} The contraction--treewidth correspondence is
Markov--Shi~\cite{markov2008simulating}; grid brambles are due to Seymour and
Thomas~\cite{seymour1993graph}, with multidimensional grid values by
Kozawa--Otachi--Yamazaki~\cite{kozawa2014treewidth} and the pathwidth--treewidth relation
quantified by Groenland et al.~\cite{groenland2023tight}. On the solver side, exact-treewidth
implementations in the PACE-challenge line now attach compact minor-based certificates to
the lower bounds they compute~\cite{tamaki2022heuristic}; those certificates are validated
by unverified code, which is precisely the trust gap our bramble validation closes. Our
contribution here is not new graph theory but the first certified form of its
pathwidth lower-bound machinery, engineered so that only interval Helly is
needed.

\paragraph{Escapes from the width paradigm.} Decision diagrams beat width on structured
values~\cite{dd2025treewidth}, and shallow random 2D circuits are efficiently
simulable~\cite{napp2022efficient}. Both shape our statements: the compression floor is
explicitly \emph{generic}, and the regime hypothesis lives in the routing layer.

\paragraph{Formalization.} Graph-theory-in-proof-assistant precedents include the Coq
library of Doczkal and Pous~\cite{doczkal2020graph} (minors, the treewidth-two
characterization) and Dittmann's Isabelle/HOL tree decompositions~\cite{dittmann2016tree},
which prove the treewidth of trees and of complete graphs; neither formalizes pathwidth,
duality certificates for structured graphs, or any link to contraction. On the tensor
side, TNLean formalizes the fundamental theorem of matrix product states~\cite{tnlean2026},
again without contraction complexity. To our knowledge this is the first pathwidth
lower-bound theory and the first TNCO development in any proof assistant. In spirit our
certificates follow verified SAT/LP checking, where cake\_lpr validates untrusted UNSAT
proofs with a verified checker~\cite{tan2021cakelpr}: the search is untrusted, the
validation is verified, and brambles play for width the role dual solutions play for LP
bounds.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}\label{sec:limits}

Certificate scope, stated as claims a user may rely on. (1)~The unconditional floors
quantify over stem (sweep) orders; the extension to arbitrary contraction trees is the
$\pw=\Theta(\tw)$ lattice input, and the development contains no type of tree contraction
orders: the conditional tree statement lives in prose only, and its named-\lstinline|Prop|
form holds on our model vacuously (the order space is all stems). Formalizing subtree
Helly and tree decompositions would make it a theorem, and is the natural next step. Today's
certificate reads: no sweep-style order beats $54$; conditionally, and outside the
formalization, no contraction tree beats $\Theta(54)$. (2)~The instantiated lattice is the deep-regime wire-chain spacetime
grid; the $(2{+}1)$-dimensional chip-lattice bramble~\cite{kozawa2014treewidth} is future
work, and for general irregular networks the framework already applies
(any bramble, however found, yields a certified floor), so curating brambles for practical networks is
a tooling problem rather than a theory gap. (3)~\lstinline|orderCost| counts materialized
boundary-tensor entries; constant-factor FLOP models and slicing trade-offs sit above this
floor (slicing exchanges memory for time, not total work). (4)~The compression floor is
generic by design: structured instances (stabilizer, genuinely low-rank) can and should
beat it, and concrete circuits over discrete gate sets are not covered by a
vanishing-set (generic) statement; it is also a separate certificate, stated outside the
headline conjunction: no theorem composes it
with the \lstinline|orderCost| floor, and the benchmark witness family is constant in the
gate parameters (\S\ref{sec:endtoend}). (5)~The measure-zero corollary ($\Pr[\text{collapse}]=0$) is
stated but not yet formalized. (6)~The identification of stem contraction orders with path
decompositions (\S\ref{sec:background}), of \lstinline|orderCost| with contraction
work, and of the full $N\times N$ grid graph with the sparser gate-level network graph of a
deep brickwork chain, are modeling correspondences argued in prose; the formal order space
\emph{is} the path decompositions of the grid graph. (7)~The empirical comparison (\S\ref{sec:empirical}) reports fixed search budgets, with a
sensitivity sweep at $128/512/4096$ repeats: the grid drifts close to within the
conversion allowance as the budget grows, while the 2D-chip deficits persist ($+6$ to
$+10$ at $4096$); observed gaps measure search quality at the stated budget, not limit
behavior. (8)~The routing scaffolding certifies one direction of the lightcone boundary:
explicit walks witness reachability for $i < d$ (\lstinline|bond_causal|); the converse
distance lower bound (unreachability for $i \ge d$) is not formalized.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

TNCO search now has an absolute yardstick: a machine-checked cost floor (bramble dual
certificates for width, an explicit cost semantics, and, alongside them, a framework-level
generic no-compression theorem on the rank side whose benchmark instantiation certifies
capability only, \S\ref{sec:endtoend}), with an axiom footprint of exactly
\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, \texttt{Quot.sound}. On the calibration
instance the yardstick is exact in width: the certified floor meets the explicit sweep at
$54$, a machine-checked optimality proof among stem orders. The remaining work extends
the scope of the certificates without touching their trusted base: tree orders via
subtree Helly, chip lattices, and bramble libraries for the irregular networks where
heuristics currently fly blind.
